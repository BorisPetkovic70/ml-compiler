\section{Introduction}

This tutorial is meant for developers and engineers who want to understand how modern ML compilers work
under the hood, design their own IRs, and experiment with lowering, rewriting, and optimization 
passes in a practical, hands-on way.

We will use xDSL, a Python-based framework for building and experimenting with compiler intermediate
representations (IRs) inspired by MLIR. It lets you define dialects, operations, types, and
transformation passes directly in Python, without having to dive into C++ or the full LLVM/MLIR
toolchain. This makes it ideal for rapid prototyping, teaching, and research in compiler design.
